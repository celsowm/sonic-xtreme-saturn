# Technical roadmap

Baseline inspected: LibSaturn af86f8518f0d014f2a752ca43035d2278bc9bf8f. **Target stock Saturn, NTSC 320x224 at a stable 30 displayed FPS**, with gameplay physics at a fixed 60 Hz. The goal is an intentionally 30 FPS game, not a temporary concession on the way to 60 FPS. Display FPS has not been independently verified on hardware. PAL and optional RAM cart enhancements follow stock qualification. Prefer bounded data and fixed-point math; no dynamic allocation in frame hot paths.

## Performance target (decision: stable 30 FPS)

The project owner selected **30 FPS** as the definitive visual target on the stock Saturn, including completed and visually rich scenarios. Do not sacrifice the final course, textures, visual effects or readable Sonic animations merely to chase 60 FPS. A 60 Hz **fixed simulation** is retained for movement/collision/input, independent of the 30 Hz rendering cadence (nominally two simulation ticks per rendered frame).

- **Frame budget:** 33.33 ms per presented frame, *including* world/character skinning, camera, scene transform/sort, VDP1 submission/draw, HUD, audio contention and system overhead. Aim below roughly 28–30 ms in representative heavy scenes to leave a small 3–5 ms safety margin for spikes; that margin is a development goal, not a measured result.
- **Frame pacing:** prefer a regular two-field NTSC presentation cadence; investigate stutter, large p95/p99 frame times, dropped/late swaps and uneven camera motion before lowering the graphics budget.
- **Acceptance:** measure a route with dense scenery, maximum expected actors/effects, busy camera views, running/jumping and HUD enabled/disabled. Compare sustained cadence, frame-time distribution, face/command counts, VDP1/VDP2/WRAM budgets and physics replay determinism. Qualify on Ymir and Mednafen, and independently verify displayed frames on physical Saturn; the XTDB `submission_rate_hz` field is a **submission rate**, not proof of actual display FPS.
- **Current evidence:** the repository's Sonic R LOD-160 single-case Ymir idle probe passed at about 30 scene submissions/s, with 159 faces, 125 skinned vertices and 148 textures, but this is *not* a stress test of a finished level. The earlier greybox/proxy rendering at 60 submissions/s is historical headroom evidence, **not a target**.
- Prioritize stable gameplay and better visuals over speculative simplification. Only create or select more aggressive LODs when worst-case scene measurements or VRAM constraints show they are needed.

## Visual rework following user review — contiguous Jade Gully v2

The user rejected the visual quality of the previous four-case Adventure PASS: it resembled a Sonic Mars/32X-era floating-path prototype, not a contiguous action-adventure world. Gameplay correctness alone is not acceptance. [Art acceptance specification](JADE_GULLY_ART_DIRECTION.md).

Normal Adventure rendering uses a closer 7m/3.5m/60° chase camera, starts with debug HUD hidden, draws simple eight-sided gold ring silhouettes instead of colored editor diamonds, and uses conservative AABB distance culling for scenery (not collision). The earlier, qualified classic layout remains untouched. CMake chooses Adventure-only camera/rendering via `XTREME_STAGE_LAYOUT`. The storyboard and 30 FPS visual target remain.

**Not yet qualified:** this v2 change has not been measured in a post-change Ymir capture, Mednafen or real hardware. Evaluate new images before claiming improved art; use targeted off-road and new route traversals in addition to the previous four smoke cases. The 30 submissions/s, 34ms p95 and zero errors cited for the earlier Adventure variant are baseline evidence, not results for this v2 candidate.

## Committed CC0 indexed8 terrain texture pipeline (2026-11-20)

The original temporary generated ground noise texture data was replaced by **eight unchanged Kenney Retro Textures Fantasy CC0 64×64 source PNGs** committed to `assets/textures/jade-gully/`. See [exact artwork and licence provenance](../assets/textures/jade-gully/ATTRIBUTION.md). Python standard library `tools/bake_stage_textures.py` decodes indexed PNGs, generates a shared RGB555 CRAM palette with 31 distinct colours per surface, and emits eight resident 64×64 VDP1 INDEX8 materials (32,768 texture bytes) into the CMake build tree. The Adventure renderer submits these real material descriptors rather than single flat RGB colours. The classic layout remains unaffected. **Authoring/host CI passed** [run 44847714151](https://github.com/celsowm/sonic-xtreme-saturn/actions/runs/44847714151) with 26 tests and deterministic texture output.

The texture build **has not been verified in the Saturn emulator or measured for actual 30 FPS displayed cadence**; the previous 5/5 and 34 ms results were obtained *before* these texture uploads and are not transferable. Next: launch the local Adventure Ymir five-case probe, inspect screenshot quality and native VRAM headroom, then address stretched UV mapping and foreground density. Sonic X-treme historical prototype texture sheets are linked as research but have not been copied into the public repo because redistribution permission was not established; the user explicitly allowed CC0 as an alternative.

## Jade Gully v4 — UV-stretch mitigation and grouped palm LOD (candidate, not yet Ymir-tested)
